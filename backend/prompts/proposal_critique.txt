Focus on whether the whole reasoning chain is research-quality, not merely well written.
Diagnose concrete weaknesses in each stage:
- Gap stage: gap validity, literature grounding, whether the gap is meaningful rather than generic future work, and risk that retrieved work already solves it.
- Scholarly positioning stage: whether the proposal critically compares prior approaches and identifies unresolved tensions rather than simply summarizing papers.
- Domain routing stage: whether the methodology family matches the research domain and avoids inappropriate template transfer.
- Research scope stage: whether population/task, condition/domain, intervention/system, duration/evaluation period, control/baseline, and outcomes are concrete enough for a PhD-level empirical study.
- Concept definition stage: whether abstract technical terms such as internal representation, interpretability, safety, trust, controllability, or fairness are operationally defined.
- Technical feasibility stage: whether required model access, hidden states, datasets, GPU resources, human participants, ethics approval, multilingual validation, and annotation protocols are realistic.
- Research question stage: researchability, scope appropriateness, variable or construct clarity, and whether the question can guide an empirical study.
- Theoretical mechanism stage: whether the mechanism explains why the effect should occur, with IV, mediator, moderator, DV, theory, explicit assumptions, and separated system-level/user-level pathways where applicable.
- Hypothesis stage: falsifiability, causal or mechanism-level grounding, measurable outcomes, and alignment with the research question.
- Operationalization and causal stage: whether every construct has a measurement instrument or behavioural log, metrics match the level of analysis, causal pathway, confounds, mediators/moderators, and limits of causal claims.
- Methodology stage: baseline/control appropriateness, metric validity, statistical or evaluation validity, reproducibility, confound control, and risk or failure-mode awareness.
- Novelty stage: whether theoretical, methodological, and practical contributions are specific and more than simply applying AI to a domain.
- Failure analysis stage: validity threats, ethical/safety risks, negative-result interpretation, and mitigation.
- Proposal stage: contribution clarity, type of novelty, coherence, limitation awareness, and academic communication.
- Cross-stage alignment: whether the gap leads to the question, the question leads to the hypothesis, the hypothesis is testable by the method, and the evaluation can answer the question.

Be specific and actionable. If a stage is strong, say why briefly; if it is weak, explain the failure mode and what should be revised.
